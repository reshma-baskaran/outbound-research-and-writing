# Real campaign pattern: event-led insurance outreach

This case is derived from a real senior-operator outreach packet created after
an insurance industry event and a related stage session. The public version
preserves the campaign logic and removes every person name, company name,
profile URL, product identity, and private campaign detail.

It is a **real drafting case**, not a performance case. The source artifact
contains seven personalised drafts. It does not contain verified sending,
delivery, reply, meeting, pipeline, or revenue outcomes, so none are claimed.

## Campaign input

| Input | Source-bounded value |
|---|---|
| Audience | Seven senior insurance operators across risk, innovation, claims, underwriting, technology, and benefits |
| Shared context | An industry event and a stage session about operating capacity and governed AI execution |
| Operating pressure | Service and claims volume rising faster than team capacity |
| Capability frame | Repeatable insurance interactions handled with system integration, controls, and auditability |
| Objective | Start a relevant peer-level conversation, not imply an active buying project |
| CTA style | Compare notes or explore where the operating pressure is highest |

## What stayed consistent

The source packet used one common campaign spine:

1. Start from a public, role-relevant observation.
2. Connect it to the event thesis without claiming the person attended the
   session or endorsed it.
3. Translate the shared thesis into the operator's functional world.
4. Describe a bounded capability rather than a guaranteed outcome.
5. Close with a low-friction conversation request.

## What changed by buyer

| Senior role | Public research angle | Role-specific operating problem | Message job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enterprise risk leader | Infrastructure risk and active risk management | Introduce automation without creating new governance exposure | Connect execution capacity to controls |
| Innovation leader | Experimentation and organisational learning | Move promising AI work from tests into repeatable operations | Bridge innovation and operating capacity |
| Claims leader | Claims and catastrophe operations | Absorb volume while preserving judgement, consistency, and documentation | Explore claims-capacity pressure |
| Claims technology leader | Customer expectations and claims technology | Improve triage, speed, and record quality at higher volume | Connect workflow execution to claims quality |
| Underwriting operations leader | Quality, controls, and change management | Support intake, document review, and follow-up without replacing expert judgement | Frame governed underwriting assistance |
| Enterprise technology leader | Modernisation, data foundations, and enterprise value | Turn AI capability into measurable operating leverage across systems | Elevate the discussion to architecture and scale |
| Benefits business leader | Small-business resilience and service burden | Reduce repetitive service load while keeping support available | Tie automation to employer servicing |

The personalisation is not a swapped first line. Each variant changes the
operating problem, capability emphasis, proof requirement, and reason for the
conversation.

## Claim lineage used in the drafts

| Claim type | Allowed source | Safe use |
|---|---|---|
| Recipient interest or remit | Public profile or public post reviewed for the draft | Attribute only the visible topic; do not infer an internal initiative |
| Event thesis | Approved event or stage-session material | Describe the thesis as the sender's point of view |
| Workflow examples | Approved capability material | Use as examples of what the system can support, not proof of deployment at the account |
| Account need | No verified account source in the packet | Keep conditional or omit |
| Buying intent, budget, timing | No source | Prohibited |

## Message anatomy

The seven real drafts followed this shape:

```text
Hi [first name],

[One public observation tied to the person's role.]

[The event thesis: an operating pressure and a bounded execution model.]

[A role-specific translation of that pressure and capability.]

[One low-friction question or invitation to compare notes.]

[Sender-derived signoff]
```

The greeting and signoff shown here are templates. The original packet used
real names; the public repository does not publish them.

## What this case proves

- One shared campaign thesis was translated into seven distinct senior-buyer
  angles.
- The variation happened at the problem, workflow, proof, and CTA layers—not
  only in the opening sentence.
- The drafts separated public observations from unverified account need.
- The method can be reused without exposing the original recipients or copy.

## Outcome boundary

The retained source proves that seven personalised drafts were prepared. It
does **not** establish that they were sent or that they produced replies,
meetings, pipeline, or revenue. Those states remain unknown unless a separate
authoritative campaign source is approved for publication.
